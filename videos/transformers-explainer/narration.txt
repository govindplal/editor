Type, the cat sat on the... and an AI finishes it: mat. But how does it know? Meet the Transformer.
First, your sentence gets chopped into pieces, called tokens. Each token gets an ID number.
Every token then becomes a long list of numbers: a point on a giant map of meaning. Cat lands near dog, and far away from car.
Word order matters too, so each token also gets a stamp, saying where it sits in the line.
Now, the magic part. Attention. Every word looks at every other word, and asks: who matters to me? Sat looks hard at cat, because cats do the sitting, and barely glances at the.
Here's how. Each word sends out a question, called a query. Every other word holds up a label, called a key. Where they match, the word soaks up their value.
And it does this many times in parallel. One head tracks grammar. Another, meaning. Another, position.
Then it all gets stacked, layer upon layer. Big models repeat this nearly a hundred times, understanding a little deeper on every floor.
At the top, the model scores every word it knows. Mat, sixty two percent. Sofa, eighteen. Roof, nine. It picks one.
Then the new word joins the sentence, and the whole thing runs again. One word at a time. That's a Transformer.
